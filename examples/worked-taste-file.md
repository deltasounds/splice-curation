# A worked taste file

> **Read this for shape, not for content.** This is one curator's taste file, developed
> over real sessions and de-identified for publication. It is here to show what a
> *filled-in* file looks like — how deep the disqualifiers go, how signatures separate
> from profiles, what a global disqualifier layer does — because the blank template can't
> show that.
>
> **Do not adopt it.** A framework whose whole premise is *your* judgment fails the moment
> you inherit someone else's. The value is in the structure; the content has to be yours.
> Start from [`personal/my-taste.example.md`](../personal/my-taste.example.md) and write
> your own — this file is the worked answer you check your shape against, not a preset.

Note what's *absent*, too: the original carries artist references and a specific gear
list. Those belong in a private file. Here they're rendered as descriptors, which is both
publishable and more portable — descriptors transfer between people, names don't.

---

## Governing idea

> I am not building a library of the music I want to make.
> I am building a library of **ingredients** that could become it.

One sentence at the top, before any profile. It's the tiebreaker for every close call
below, and it's why "finished and excellent" loses to "unfinished and strange" throughout.

Organized around **source character for transformation**, not genre.

## Reference field

Descriptors rather than names — what the ancestry *sounds like*, so it stays usable by
someone who doesn't share the references:

- Off-grid sample chopping with heavy, human swing
- Source material transformed past recognition
- Sound design that carries the arrangement; tracks that develop like a narrative
- Microscopic detail in the percussion and texture layers
- Melodic restraint — emotional weight without obvious hooks
- Broken-beat pocket
- Nocturnal, degraded, rain-soaked mood

The point is to have these in the ancestry **without sounding like any of them.**

## The collection test

Applied on top of any profile match. A profile filters; this decides.

1. **Does it contain a distinctive sound, rhythm, texture, or fragment I could transform?**
2. **Does it give me possibilities rather than finish the job for me?** — negative space;
   incomplete propositions beat finished loops.
3. **Does it make me curious?** — a human, review-lane call. If I instantly know exactly
   how to use it, it may be less interesting than something that makes me wonder.

Guiding principle: **character over polish, specificity over generic perfection, source
over product.**

## Contrast matters

Not everything should be degraded. Clean, intimate, or highly detailed material is
valuable *when it has distinctive character* — a pristine vibraphone, an extraordinary
clean field recording. Degradation can happen downstream; it rarely needs buying. The
interesting territory is the collision:

```
organic ↔ synthetic   clean ↔ degraded   precise ↔ loose
beautiful ↔ abrasive   melodic ↔ textural
```

This section exists to stop a well-tuned signature from converging the whole library.

## Global disqualifiers — the anti-library

Applies to **every** search, above per-profile rules.

> Fill in your own instrument and effect list here. The rule that generates this section:
> **don't spend credits on what your gear already makes.** You can't produce someone
> else's room, tape machine, or drummer — you can produce another synth loop.

- Finished/mastered melodic loops and construction kits
- Generic chord progressions and recognizable genre presets
- Generic risers, sweeps, and "drop" FX
- Huge sets of near-identical clean electronic kicks
- Heavily processed drum loops — the processing is the part I want to do
- Polished synth loops reproducible on gear I own
- Anything that already sounds like a finished record

## Credit / effort mix

A guardrail for **where to spend credits and effort** — not a target for file counts.
Drum one-shots multiply fast; 50 great breaks beat 2,000 one-shots; 300 strange field
recordings isn't overcollection. Use for priority, never to force percentages.

```
50%  Rhythmic material (breaks, drum loops, percussion, one-shots)
20%  Organic / musical source (instrumental fragments)
15%  Texture / field / foley
10%  Vocals (fragments, not songs)
 5%  Unusual electronic source material
```

## Character signatures

Vibes as *character*, not genre. Each says what it includes and what disqualifies it.
Signatures are referenced by profiles; they aren't tied to one function.

### Dusty & degraded
```
Includes:      Vinyl/tape character, saturation, audible noise floor, gentle HF rolloff.
Disqualifiers: Clean/pristine when that's all it is, loudness-maximized, sterile.
```
One option among the collision — not the default. See "Contrast matters."

### Organic & imperfect
```
Includes:      Real players, room sound, acoustic artifacts, ghost notes — raw material
               with character to push against.
Disqualifiers: Over-edited, gridded-perfect, obviously programmed.
```

### Loose & pocketed
```
Includes:      Human push/pull, swing, ghost-note interplay, asymmetry, syncopation,
               behind/ahead-of-beat feel, a distinctive pocket.
Disqualifiers: Rigid grid, generic four-on-the-floor drive, mechanically quantized,
               busy without groove.
```
Distinct from "organic" — an acoustic drummer can be organic with dead pocket.

### Textural & spatial
```
Includes:      Grain, noise, ambience, movement inside sustained sound, reverb/space as
               a compositional element.
Disqualifiers: Static pads, generic presets, clean and lifeless, "beautiful" but inert.
```

### Precise & detailed
```
Includes:      Refined sound design, clarity, high-resolution detail with distinctive
               character.
Disqualifiers: Generic-pristine, sample-library-clean, character-free polish.
```

### Glitch & micro
```
Includes:      Digital errors, granular bursts, buffer glitches, precise micro-edits,
               synthetic percussive artifacts.
Disqualifiers: Loose organic swing, cheesy, jammy, warm-nostalgic.
```

Emotional register throughout: melancholy, warmth, tension, ambiguity — not obvious hooks.

## Profiles

Each carries a `Transformation` read and a `Good for` list, so affordance is weighed
separately from liking the source.

### 1. Breaks & drum loops — highest priority
```
Name:           Unprocessed real-drummer break
Function:       Loop (drum break)
Character:      Loose & pocketed / Organic & imperfect
Includes:       Real kit or hand percussion with distinctive pocket. Ancestry: funk,
                soul, jazz, afrobeat, brazilian, dub, broken beat, jazz-funk, neo-soul,
                garage, 2-step, electro, restrained jungle & breakbeat.
Disqualifiers:  Already heavily processed, clean/quantized, rigidly tight, mastered-loud,
                busy without pocket.
Constraints:    loop; note BPM.
Transformation: high
Good for:       slicing, rearrangement, transient extraction, off-grid chopping
Default lane:   review
Notes:          Weight broken beat — clearest bridge between hip-hop, jazz/soul, electronic.
```

### 2. Drum one-shots
```
Name:           Character drum one-shot
Function:       Kick / snare / hat / percussion (one-shot)
Character:      Any distinctive — degraded OR clean-but-characterful
Includes:       Dry acoustic or dusty hits, rimshots, brushes, ghost notes, hand/wood/
                metal percussion, vinyl-derived hits, unusual found percussion.
Disqualifiers:  Near-identical sub kicks, clean electronic kicks, over-distorted hits.
Constraints:    one-shot; dry.
Transformation: medium
Good for:       layering, sound design, sampler kits
Default lane:   trusted
Notes:          Batch-approve after a scan. Character over quantity — don't hoard.
```

### 3. Organic instrumental fragments
```
Name:           Incomplete instrumental phrase
Function:       Melodic / harmonic loop or one-shot
Character:      Organic & imperfect / Precise & detailed
Includes:       Small incomplete phrases — two-chord electric piano, strange guitar
                harmonic, single horn swell, unresolved piano chord. Wurlitzer, vibes,
                upright bass, mallets, organ, strings. A beautifully recorded
                vibraphone counts.
Disqualifiers:  Full arrangements, resolved/complete progressions, session-perfect-generic.
Constraints:    short; key optional.
Transformation: high
Good for:       chopping, repitching, harmonic reframing, reversing, granulation
Default lane:   review
Notes:          The value is the fragment that leaves somewhere to go.
```

### 4. Vocal fragments
```
Name:           Transformable vocal fragment
Function:       Vocal (one-shot / texture)
Character:      Dusty & degraded / Textural & spatial / Precise & detailed
Includes:       Soul/gospel/jazz phrases, wordless singing, hums, breaths, single
                syllables, choral fragments, formant-shifted/granular voices, vocal pads.
Disqualifiers:  Full toplines, clean modern pop vocals, intelligible lead delivery.
Constraints:    short.
Transformation: high
Good for:       pitching, formant-shifting, granulation, layering
Default lane:   review
Notes:          A half-second fragment turned into an instrument beats a whole topline.
```

### 5a. Environmental source (field) — high priority, high diversity
```
Name:           Environmental field recording
Function:       Texture / ambience
Character:      Textural & spatial
Includes:       Rooms, transit, streets, weather, forests, crowds, distant voices,
                machinery, doors/metal/glass/wood, scrapes, footsteps, household.
Disqualifiers:  "Musical" polished ambience, generic FX-pack sweeps, obviously synthetic.
Constraints:    none usually.
Transformation: high
Good for:       granulation, convolution, layering
Default lane:   review
Notes:          Worth collecting broadly. The best ones don't sound musical.
```

### 5b. Media patina — collect sparingly
```
Name:           Recorded-media patina
Function:       Texture (noise bed)
Character:      Dusty & degraded
Includes:       Tape hiss, cassette/reel artifacts, vinyl crackle, radio static,
                electrical noise, old recording artifacts.
Disqualifiers:  Loud/gimmicky, obviously looped.
Constraints:    short beds.
Transformation: low–medium
Good for:       layering under material, downstream degradation
Default lane:   trusted
Notes:          A HANDFUL of excellent ones is enough — don't collect 300 vinyl crackles.
                Low transformation means the category is small by definition.
```

### 6. Tonal one-shots
```
Name:           Tonal one-shot (instrument-able)
Function:       One-shot (tonal)
Character:      Organic & imperfect / Precise & detailed / Textural & spatial
Includes:       Bells, mallets, plucks, kalimba, vibes, piano/electric-piano notes,
                guitar harmonics, prepared piano, glass, wood, metallic resonances.
Disqualifiers:  Generic synth plucks I could make, clean sampled-instrument libraries.
Constraints:    one-shot.
Transformation: high
Good for:       pitching one sample across a keyboard as a playable instrument
Default lane:   trusted
Notes:          One good source note = an instrument nobody else has.
```

### 7. Glitch / micro-sound
```
Name:           Glitch / micro fragment
Function:       Percussion / FX (one-shot / fill)
Character:      Glitch & micro
Includes:       Digital errors, buffer glitches, clicks, stutters, bit-crushed fragments,
                circuit noise, granular bursts, reversed transients, tiny vocal fragments.
Disqualifiers:  Warm organic hits, jammy, anything that grooves on its own.
Constraints:    one-shot or short.
Transformation: low (often ready to use)
Good for:       direct placement, layering, micro-rhythm
Default lane:   trusted
Notes:          MANUFACTURE these too — resample my own material through granular and
                distortion processing, then chop. Don't over-buy what I can make.
```

### 8. Drones & beds
```
Name:           Moving drone / bed
Function:       Texture / ambience (sustained)
Character:      Textural & spatial / Precise & detailed
Includes:       Analog/acoustic drones, bowed strings/metal, feedback, granular beds,
                dark-ambient beds, tape loops, reversed ambience — with movement inside.
Disqualifiers:  Static pads, inert presets, generic "cinematic" beds.
Constraints:    long/sustained.
Transformation: medium
Good for:       resampling, modulation, spectral processing
Default lane:   review
Notes:          Evolution within the sustain, not a flat wash.
```

### 9. Wildcard / strange source — low volume, review only
```
Name:           Wildcard / strange source
Function:       Unknown / transformation source
Character:      Any, but distinctive
Includes:       Sounds whose use isn't obvious; unusual instruments, strange
                performances, accidental noises, unfamiliar material, awkward fragments,
                artifacts that provoke curiosity.
Disqualifiers:  Generic novelty FX, comedy sounds, obvious production clichés.
Transformation: unknown (that's the point)
Good for:       whatever it becomes
Default lane:   review
Notes:          Guards against the taxonomy trap — a profile set can only find what I've
                already imagined. Keep the volume LOW so "it's weird" doesn't become an
                excuse to accumulate. Curiosity is the test, and it's mine to apply.
```

converge on your actual taste rather than your first guess at it.
